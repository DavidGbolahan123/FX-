      How the score works

      Weights and thresholds

      Macro score. Each currency is scored from the rate path priced for the next year (30%), real policy rate (20%), yield level (15%), labour trend (15%), inflation momentum (10%) your tilt (10%) and headline tone (6%, a rough keyword read of the latest central bank and FX headlines). A pair's macro score is the base currency score minus the quote currency score, on a −100 to +100 scale.

      Technical score. Trend from the 20, 50 and 200 EMAs (35%), momentum from MACD and RSI (25%), structure from the 55-bar range and 20-bar breakouts (25%), and 20-bar rate of change in ATR units (15%). Bars are 4 hours, so 20 bars is about 3 trading days.

      Signal. Combined score is the average of the two. Strong needs both scores to agree and the average above 45. Moderate needs it above 25. When macro and chart point opposite ways the pair is marked Conflict and should be skipped.

      Trade levels. Stop sits beyond the 10-bar swing, kept between 1.2 and 2.5 ATR from the close. Targets are 1.5R and 3R. Treat the close as a reference entry, and prefer a pullback toward the 20 EMA when the page says the pair is extended.

      Macro inputs come from central bank statements, official statistics and broker previews published up to 23 Oct 2026, and they go stale as new data lands. This is a decision aid for research and content, not personal financial advice. Size positions against your own risk limits.
